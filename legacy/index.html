    How the rails work

    Not strict. There to keep us on course.

    A week, 4 × 50 min + Saturday

    • Monday10-minute all-hands. Read this week’s page out loud, put a name next to every task, check for a new Team Update or Q&A answer.
    • Tue–WedWork. Builders are on call for coders; coders tell builders what they find. Everything touched goes in the notes.
    • ThursdayDemo day. Show every “Done when” box. Tick only what you showed. Triage the driver wish list. Each team shares one thing it learned with the other team.
    • FridayFive minutes: check the heads-up list, update the notes, use “Copy status” to post the week in team chat.
    • SaturdayOptional, 3 hours: the deep-work task, plus driver practice. Never the only chance to get something done.

    The catch-up ladder

    1. Carry-over first. Unticked demos from earlier weeks come before new work, unless the week page says they can be cut.
    2. Cut from the “If you’re behind” line. Every week names what to drop and what never to drop.
    3. Borrow people. Builders can run drive tests, coders can wire, drivers can build the practice field.
    4. Protect three things: drivers’ time on the robot, the next gate, and a release that installs without Wi-Fi.

    Drifting is normal. Staying quiet about it isn’t. Say it Monday, not on the day of the event.

    Rules that never change

    Whole team
    • If it isn’t written down, it didn’t happen. A notes entry every session. That’s the portfolio.
    • Change one thing at a time and write down what happened.
    • Eye protection whenever a shooter runs. Nobody stands in front of a flywheel.
    • Batteries are labeled, rotated and never charged unattended.
    Build & drive
    • Drivers get the robot before anyone polishes it.
    • Accurate beats fast: 4 of 4 in 3 s beats 2 of 4 in 1 s.
    • Mechanical mods need four lines: what, why, how we’ll test it, how we roll it back. If it can’t roll back before the next event, don’t.
    • Seven days before an event: repairs only. No new mechanisms, no new features.
    Code
    • Code ends every session on the robot.
    • No waiting in robot code. A robot that waits can’t steer.
    • Every PR says what you didn’t test.
    • Every release installs offline and can be rolled back by a second person.
    Why the season is shaped this way. Robot Day is Sunday Nov 1, so the first two weeks are a sprint to a working v1. The scrimmage on Dec 12 is the dress rehearsal, and winter break sits between it and the qualifier. So we tag a release candidate on Friday Dec 18, use the first week of January only to fix what full matches show, and freeze on Friday Jan 8. Qualifier week is for driver practice, judging practice and packing. Ball-finding vision, a second Auto cycle and speed come after.

    How we win: the strategy rails
    • 1. Never lose a ranking point to Auto. Leave and park, every match, from every start spot. It’s 8 points per robot and, if we’re reading the Swarm rule right, a ranking point when both robots do it.
    • 2. Tips are the game. 20 points each, each one unlocks another nectar, and tip counts decide two more ranking points. Measure: how many seconds from first pickup to a tip?
    • 3. Accuracy over speed. A missed shot wastes an element that someone else can tip with. Hit rate from fixed spots comes before cycle time.
    • 4. Don’t hand points away. Four-element limit, no opponent nectar, no early nectar. Drivers know these cold before the scrimmage.
    • 5. Expect friendly defense. The game is highly interactive. Practice with a teammate defending, and decide what each team does about it (2827’s traction mode exists for this).
    • 6. Flowers are optional. Both teams are skipping them for now. Revisit after the qualifier with real match data, not hunches.
    What other teams are learning about vision
    • The tags move. Each Hive cell has a 4-tag cluster on its underside, facing down, and it tips with the Hive (manual 9.6, 9.9). The tags can’t be fixed landmarks the way they were in past seasons.
    • Odometry does the localizing. On Chief Delphi most teams use Pinpoint for position and keep tags for aiming and for telling which cell is up. One team reports Pinpoint back within about 1 in after random driving.
    • A wrong fix is worse than a late one. Mona Shores only corrects the pose from a cell that has settled, and pauses corrections during a tip.
    • Limelight’s full-field pose is fragile here. All 8 pipelines share one field map, you need a red and a blue map, and there’s no filter for upside-down tags. Rocket Robotics (21615) published a Python cluster pipeline instead, which gives range, bearing and roll to the cell’s center.
    • Cheap drift fix. Several teams suggest a driver button that resets the pose when the robot is pushed into a known corner.
    • Balls: color first. A color pipeline runs at about 90 fps on a Limelight 3A; a trained detector at about 11 fps. Yellow pollen is the easiest to find. Red nectar gets confused with tape, panels and skin, and shadows hurt the simple color filter most.
    • One tilt, one job. Tags hang above and face down; balls sit on the floor. Pick what each camera is for. (3587 is doing it with two cameras.)

    Where this format comes from

    Each week gets one plan, split into build & design, programming and outreach. Drive & strategy, gates, and a rails check sit on top.

    • Test several ideas before committing. Try more than one intake or launcher before anyone falls for the first thing that works. Nothing gets cut in final parts until it’s been tested in cardboard or quick-build.
    • Programming runs on a test robot early, with pathing and driver automations coming together before the real robot is finished. That’s why code is written before Robot Day.
    • Outreach and documentation are part of the plan, not a scramble in January. Written goals, a master season-notes document, and portfolio pages that grow every week.
